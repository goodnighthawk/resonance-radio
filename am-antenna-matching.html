ANTENNAS

AM antenna matching for electrically short antennas

Published September 27, 2026 · Resonance Radio

At medium-wave frequencies, a legal short antenna is nowhere near a quarter wavelength. Matching is therefore the heart of the system.

Why the antenna looks capacitive

A short vertical conductor stores more electric-field energy than it radiates, so its feed impedance contains a large capacitive reactance. A loading inductor can cancel much of that reactance at the operating frequency.

The practical tuning method

Start with low RF power. Adjust the loading inductance or tuning capacitor for a clear peak in a repeatable received-signal indicator. Then verify that the final-stage current remains within the intended operating point and that harmonics remain controlled.

Do not optimize by ear alone

A louder radio can result from receiver AGC, local noise changes or coupling into mains wiring. Use fixed geometry and an SDR trace when possible.

Losses dominate quickly

Coil series resistance, thin wire, poor connections and lossy nearby materials can consume a large fraction of the available RF power. A larger, lower-resistance coil can improve efficiency without changing transmitter power.

What we will measure

For Resonance Radio experiments we will log coil inductance, DC resistance, operating frequency, antenna length and received amplitude at fixed locations. That makes antenna changes comparable instead of anecdotal.

For U.S. Part 15 operation under §15.219, remember that transmission line + antenna + ground lead together are constrained to 3 m.

Keep the geometry boring

Repeatability beats novelty. Mount the antenna in the same place, keep nearby metal objects fixed, and mark the tuning setting for each test frequency. A loading coil sitting on a steel desk can behave differently after it is moved into a plastic enclosure. Likewise, a dangling power lead can accidentally become part of the RF system. Photograph the setup and measure the physical conductors that can carry RF current before treating a matching improvement as real.
