RECEIVER GUIDE

How to choose a cheap AM radio for transmitter testing

Published September 27, 2026 · Resonance Radio

The receiver is part of the measurement chain. A $15 radio can be useful, but only if you understand what it is telling you.

Analog dial or digital tuning?

Digital tuning is easier for repeatability: 1500 kHz today is 1500 kHz tomorrow. Analog radios can still be excellent for quick listening tests and often make weak-signal changes obvious by ear.

Useful receiver traits

  • A visible or repeatable tuning scale.
  • Battery operation, which avoids power-supply noise during tests.
  • A headphone output for recording or monitoring.
  • A ferrite-bar AM antenna rather than relying on an external wire.
  • Enough selectivity to distinguish adjacent channels.

Don't confuse AGC with field strength

Automatic gain control can make a ten-fold signal change sound surprisingly small. For comparisons, keep geometry fixed and use an SDR or receiver signal indicator when possible. The portable radio is primarily a reality check: can an ordinary listener receive intelligible audio?

Our test protocol

  1. Use fresh batteries.
  2. Mark three fixed indoor positions and one outdoor position.
  3. Keep radio orientation fixed.
  4. Play the same 30-second voice/music test file.
  5. Score intelligibility, hum, distortion and adjacent-channel leakage separately.

This gives us a repeatable way to compare inexpensive radios later without pretending a subjective listening impression is a calibrated field-strength measurement.

Use more than one receiver eventually

A single radio can hide problems. One receiver may have unusually aggressive AGC, a narrow audio filter or a strong internal noise source. Once the basic station works, repeat the same route with a second radio of a different design. If both receivers show the same improvement after a transmitter or antenna change, confidence in the result increases. If they disagree, that disagreement is useful data and often points to receiver selectivity or sensitivity rather than transmitter performance.
