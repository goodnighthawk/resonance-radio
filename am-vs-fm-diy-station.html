AM vs FM for a small DIY station: different engineering problems

Published September 27, 2026 · Resonance Radio

AM and FM are not simply two ways of doing the same small transmitter project. Their antennas, noise behavior, receivers and U.S. Part 15 rules differ substantially.

Why AM is interesting

Medium-wave AM makes antenna matching visible. A very short antenna forces you to work with loading inductance, loss resistance and ground coupling. It is an excellent hands-on RF experiment and ordinary radios are plentiful.

Why FM can sound easier

FM receivers offer much wider audio bandwidth and strong rejection of amplitude noise. Antennas are also physically much closer to practical fractions of a wavelength. But the applicable unlicensed field-strength limits and compliance method are different from the AM §15.219 route.

Choose based on the experiment

If the goal is high-fidelity local audio, FM has obvious attractions. If the goal is learning RF matching, building a distinctive retro station and making use of ubiquitous AM receivers, AM is unusually satisfying.

Our choice

Resonance Radio is starting with AM because the constraints make the engineering measurable. We can characterize transmitter power, loading networks, receiver sensitivity and noise without needing a large station.

This is not a claim that AM offers greater legal range. Any unlicensed design should be evaluated under the rules applicable to its band and configuration.

Do not transfer assumptions between bands

An antenna trick, transmitter setting or legal limit that is appropriate for medium-wave AM cannot simply be copied to FM. The wavelengths differ by roughly two orders of magnitude, receiver bandwidths are different, and the applicable Part 15 provisions use different compliance metrics. Treat any future FM build as a separate design and measurement problem rather than an “FM version” of the AM transmitter.
