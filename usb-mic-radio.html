AUDIO GUIDE

What matters in a cheap USB microphone for radio voice work

Published September 27, 2026 · Resonance Radio

For AM voice, intelligibility and noise control matter more than an impressive microphone specification sheet.

Cardioid usually helps

A directional microphone can reduce computer fan and room reflections compared with a tiny omnidirectional laptop mic. But placement matters more than price: moving a microphone from 60 cm to 10–15 cm from the speaker dramatically improves the voice-to-room ratio.

Features worth paying for

  • USB class-compliant operation on Linux.
  • Physical gain or mute control.
  • Headphone monitoring if you plan to speak live.
  • A stable stand or boom that keeps distance consistent.

Features we do not need initially

192 kHz sample rates and elaborate RGB lighting add little to an AM voice chain. A clean 48 kHz USB stream is already far beyond the transmitted audio bandwidth.

Our eventual comparison

We will compare microphones using the same mouth-to-mic distance, room, gain target and spoken passage. We will publish raw recordings as well as the audio after the station's normal processing, because a mic that looks best on paper may not sound best through the actual transmitter.

For version one

Do not buy a microphone at all if prerecorded audio is enough. Prove the transmitter first; add live voice when there is a reason.

Room treatment can be nearly free

For spoken voice, microphone position and room reflections often dominate the difference between inexpensive USB microphones. Record away from bare walls, keep the microphone close enough that room sound is secondary, and use soft furnishings already in the room before buying acoustic foam. A pop filter or slightly off-axis placement can reduce plosives. These changes are cheap, reversible and easy to compare through the actual AM chain.
