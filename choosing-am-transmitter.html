BUYING GUIDE

How to choose a low-cost AM transmitter without buying twice

Published September 27, 2026 · Resonance Radio

Cheap AM transmitter listings often emphasize maximum output power. For a small Part 15-oriented station, stability, clean modulation and controllability matter more.

1. Frequency control

Prefer a synthesised or otherwise stable carrier over an oscillator that drifts noticeably as the board warms. A display is convenient, but actual frequency should still be checked with an SDR or counter.

2. Accessible power adjustment

The useful transmitter is one that can be operated conservatively. A board with an adjustable RF stage is easier to characterize than one whose only specification is a headline wattage.

3. Audio input that is easy to drive

A 3.5 mm line-level input is ideal for our first build because a laptop or USB audio adapter can feed it directly. Avoid adding a mixer until the transmitter itself is proven clean.

4. Documentation and test points

Look for a schematic, component labels, or at minimum an identifiable final transistor and supply path. The ability to measure final-stage DC input is far more useful than a seller's unverified range claim.

5. Spectral cleanliness

A cheap board can still be useful if its harmonics are adequately filtered. Budget for an SDR-based sanity check. If the board produces strong harmonics or unstable sidebands, replacing or filtering it is more productive than increasing antenna length.

Current project choice

Our sub-$100 build uses an adjustable 530–1600 kHz board as a development platform. We are treating seller specifications as claims to verify, not measurements.

What we would pay extra for

Better frequency synthesis, documented filtering, modulation control and accessible measurement points. We would not pay extra merely for a larger advertised power number.

A five-minute screening test after delivery

Before modifying a new board, photograph it, record the supply specification, identify the RF output path and check whether any transistor becomes abnormally hot at low settings. Feed a clean tone, tune a nearby receiver and confirm that the carrier is stable enough to remain centered on the channel. If an SDR is available, compare the carrier with and without modulation and look for strong harmonics. A board that fails this baseline is a poor platform regardless of its advertised maximum range.
